# M4-G15 brief: Tolerance requests against stated tolerances (PMI)

Read with `M4-common.md`. Stub: `papers/2026-10-17-geometry-lifecycle-map.md` §4.1 G15. Bead: `brepgpu-12s.16`.

**Purpose.** Tolerance is a request, never a physical absolute, and the
operator calls metrology interpretation one of the densest sources of trouble.
Decide how the mesher reads semantic PMI, general tolerances and the nominal-
model flag to warn about ε, and how it reports the one-sided bias on features
of size and the combined source-plus-chord bound.

**Decision it blocks.** M5 §4.1 and §9.6.

**Operator decisions that bind this lane.** D-PMI: read PMI to warn when ε is
loose against the tightest stated tolerance or tighter than the source's own
uncertainty. Warn and comply, never refuse.

**Questions.**
1. The PMI subset to read: semantic PMI in AP242, general tolerances and the
   nominal-model flag, per the CAx-IF PMI recommended practice v4.1.
2. The warning rule: the ratio k between ε and the tightest relevant tolerance,
   and its basis (JCGM 106, ISO 14253-1 decision rules, which are paywalled, so
   work from secondary sources and say so).
3. Feature-of-size bias: an inscribed chord makes holes smaller and bosses
   larger. Derive the one-sided bias, and how it is reported.
4. The combined bound: source uncertainty plus chord error, and how it is
   stated to the person.
5. The NIST FTC and CTC test cases as fixtures, now in the corpus.

**Measurement.** Allowed: a text-level census of PMI and tolerances in the NIST
corpus.

**Output slug.** `pmi-tolerance-requests`: `papers/2026-10-17-pmi-tolerance-requests.md` and its sources TSV.

**Done when.** A request-checking rule, the reported quantities, and the PMI
subset read. Every question has an evidence-backed answer or an explicit "the
literature does not say" with the measurement that would settle it.

**Return.** The rule and the reported quantities. Then the common return items.
