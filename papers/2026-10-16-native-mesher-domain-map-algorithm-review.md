# Authoritative domain-map unit: scoped algorithm review

Read `docs/native-mesher-domain-map-unit.md` against the accepted whole design and the actual topology/frontier/packet seams. The proposed construction keeps original authoritative arcs and mixed-reference policy distinct from the selected polygon. Ordered B fans then disjoint T fibers give a concrete map; local inverse queries correctly sum sequential displacement contributions rather than taking one maximum across stages. Querying B supports after the inverse-T box expansion is necessary and present. Plane displacement uses lift columns with the global source height charged once in the actual-plane bound. Original extraction rejects positional discontinuities, supplying the stated piecewise-C0 Lipschitz precondition.

Scoped acceptance has two concrete integration obligations. A host domain failure found while preparing a lower batched cell must be stored on that cell and consumed only at canonical frontier priority; it cannot immediately choose a timing-dependent first boundary request. A batch containing only such host results can produce no numerical packet, so the NativeTask wrapper must requeue/re-advance instead of treating absence as an internal error. This remains the same FaceEngine/frontier, not a second mesher.

The derivative traversal sign is the sign of the interval's original spatial-t end minus begin, after composing the original t-to-pcurve-s map. It is not coedge sense alone and does not reapply curve_same_sense. Strict x-prime positivity is an explicit finite-profile restriction: stationary endpoint parameterizations may remain unresolved even when their geometric traces are regular. Subdivision/resource limits provide bounded failure; no universal termination claim follows from this profile. No new dependency, product code or test campaign was introduced by this review.

Actual-source review then read `geometry_domain.cpp`. The componentwise inverse-strip maximum, expansion before inverse-fan query, addition of the sequential fan movement, and source derivative bound over the clipped convex endpoint hull implement the proposed composition. B supports may extend outside the natural rectangle; no carrier evaluation at those ambient intermediate points is required. Construction explicitly refuses the optional authoritative PlaneXY branch before the raw-UV builder runs. An early partial-read concern about mixing raw UV and plane XY was corrected after finding that guard; it is a support gap, not an admitted wrong-result path.

Concrete constructor findings concern original source scope. Every authoritative incident endpoint must agree before an exact-A-equals-Q shortcut, and supplied mapped-s endpoints must equal the original spatial-t map. The actual revision reuses the existing authoritative-point equality and original-parameter-map helpers for both and has been reread. Supplied closed cycles alone were insufficient: source-map s values may backtrack even though its t column is strictly monotone, and a whole omitted hole leaves otherwise closed supplied cycles. The revised original face loop/coedge cursor now checks full literal spatial-t coverage, sense/order/contiguity, loop incidence and no extra/omitted uses; an arc spanning an interior mandatory map-t breakpoint refuses. Rereading those checks resolves the source-scope findings. Actual nonlinear rational fan, curved strip and mixed-reference source cases are present, with strict/sanitizer execution reported by their owner. Scoped original-UV map-constructor and inverse-bound acceptance is complete; same-producer integration and the coarse-polygon classifier remain distinct delivery work.

A remaining performance obligation was sent to the owner: original map rows are strictly monotone in t, yet the current mapped-point lookup and per-arc mandatory-break scan traverse all rows. A source with n map rows and n selected samples would do quadratic work. Binary search or one ordered admission cursor removes this known cost in the existing helper without adding a dependency or changing map semantics.
