# From selective acceleration to an owned GPU B-rep kernel

Research date: 2026-10-13. This is a proposed architecture and reading report, not an implementation or a GPU speedup claim. No library is selected. Sources and their availability limits are listed below; acquired PDFs remain ignored local research material.

The useful unit is an **intersection graph with coupled geometry, followed by a face arrangement**, rather than a batch of intersection points. A full kernel must discover every supported intersection component, represent each boundary consistently in 3D and in both incident surface domains, classify the resulting face regions, and emit valid shared topology. Selective acceleration can build that machinery incrementally without changing the output contract at each step.

## What recent work changes

Yang, Jia and Yan's 2023 algorithm separates implicitization, topology determination, tracing and parameter-domain clipping. Its author slides explicitly include isolated points, tangent curves, multiple branches and higher-order contact, and show why an implicit surface can extend beyond the original parametric patch. This supports separating branch discovery from tracing and retaining domain membership throughout. Computation efficiency remains a stated limitation. Full paper not acquired; slides are not a substitute for checking every assumption. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1145/3618349), [author slides](https://yangjieyin.github.io/homepage/ACMTOG2023/SIGA23-slides.pdf).

Cheng et al.'s 2023 interval algebraic topology analysis is another important branch-completeness route: analyze intersection topology in four parameter dimensions, then combine subdivision with marching. The full paper was not acquired in this pass, so it is an acquisition and assumption-review priority. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1145/3592452).

Yang et al.'s 2025 hybrid Boolean method maps controlled B-rep tessellations to mesh operations and then back to parametric surfaces. The slides show inflated-box and normal-range screening, numerical refinement, constrained triangulation and degeneracy handling. They also state the decisive limitation: multiple small loops inside one triangle region can escape discovery, and common intersections among multiple bodies require more work. Consequently a mesh accelerator is a strong candidate for broad phase and arrangements, but a finite mesh resolution does not alone establish general curved-intersection completeness. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1145/3730908), [author slides, especially slide 37](https://yangjieyin.github.io/homepage/SIG2025/SIG25_slides.pdf), [repository article](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1016540/files/1016540.pdf?version=1). The repository article returned an HTML challenge during acquisition; no fake PDF was retained.

Yang and Jia's 2025 overlap extraction addresses a separate two-dimensional, tolerance-based problem with bilevel point-to-surface optimization and boundary reconstruction/refinement. Its slides explicitly identify misclassification and handling transversally intersecting surfaces as limitations. Tolerance overlap is therefore not an interchangeable replacement for a transverse intersection curve or exact surface coincidence. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1145/3763308), [author slides](https://yangjieyin.github.io/homepage/SIGA2025/SigA25-slides.pdf).

Wang et al.'s watertightness work builds topology graphs in both UV domains, refines them and uses constrained optimization with positional and tangential information to reduce the gap between their surface images. This makes coupled fitting a first-class stage. The publisher says first online December 27, 2025 but supplies a 2026 citation; record both dates rather than silently relabeling it. Full article not acquired. [Publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.70298).

There is a genuinely current starting-point development: Yang and Jia's 2026 paper uses winding-number theory and subdivision of a parameter-domain vector field to find small-loop, isolated-singularity and tangent-branch starts. Publisher-deposited metadata gives TOG 45(5), published June 29, 2026; the author site still says accepted. Only metadata/abstract were read, so its claimed branch detection is a research lead requiring the full assumptions and algorithm before adoption. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1145/3807948), [publisher-deposited metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3807948), [author publication list](https://yangjieyin.github.io/publications/).

Gao, Lin and Li's April 2026 Mapper preprint is freely available and describes topology recovery from affine-arithmetic subdivision point sets. Its parameter-selection theory depends on sampling error, positive reach and convexity radius; section 4.1.1 acknowledges the singular case can lack those properties and adds subsequent handling. It is useful for graph reconstruction experiments, but an unconditional all-singularities completeness theorem cannot be inferred from its successful examples or the abstract. [Preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12631v1), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12631v1).

Exact mesh arrangements have progressed substantially. Guo and Fu (2024) localize intersection-point sorting/deduplication with indirect offset predicates; Lévy (2025) joins exact predicates, constructions and arrangement combinatorics. Study their independent cells, filtering and geometric identities. Those guarantees concern polygonal inputs; they do not automatically extend to approximated NURBS or rounded output. Valque and Lazard (2025) is an important companion on preserving floating coordinates while removing self-intersections. [Guo/Fu project](https://mangoleaves.github.io/projects/mesh-arrangements/), [Lévy paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12949), [Valque/Lazard preprint](https://inria.hal.science/hal-04907149/document).

## Proposed owned representation

These are design inferences to be pressure-tested, not claims made by the papers.

- Store surfaces, curve segments, vertices, edges, coedges, loops, faces and shells as typed native arrays with stable integer identities and ranges. GPU kernels process contiguous geometry and adjacency; device pointers never define persistent identity.
- Each intersection edge owns one 3D boundary representation and references an ordered UV curve for every incident face use. Samples/fitted segments share correspondence parameters. Preserve the original face identities, patch domains, branch IDs, endpoints, seam offsets and local orientation.
- Keep explicit statuses: excluded, regular branch, isolated contact, tangent branch, overlap region, singular event and unresolved. An iteration limit produces unresolved work with its parameter box and reason. It must never produce an empty intersection result.
- Separate geometric residual, approximation error, parametric error and topological separation. Converting a world-space tolerance into UV needs local derivative bounds; a fixed UV epsilon is unsound near stretched patches or poles.
- For the first manifold-solid scope, each regular boundary edge has two oriented face uses, with consistent endpoint identities and opposed shell traversal. Periodic faces can use one edge more than once; count uses, not distinct faces. Represent degenerate pole boundaries explicitly rather than fabricating a tiny 3D edge.

### Intersection discovery and refinement

1. Decompose rational surfaces into bounded patches; retain knot-span and parent-face provenance. Use conservative bounds on positions, weights and derivatives with stated weight/domain assumptions.
2. Batch patch-pair culling on the GPU. Bounds include approximation/tolerance inflation. Mesh intersections can generate seeds; they cannot be the only means of excluding an unresolved patch pair.
3. Subdivide ambiguous parameter boxes in queues, refining branch candidates and identifying rank-deficient or overlap cases. Certify supported exclusions and local regularity with a stated interval/predicate contract. Start with a CPU authority for hard cases while measuring how often it runs.
4. Build a branch/event graph before curve fitting. Boundary crossings, closed loops, singular junctions and isolated contacts are distinct graph entities. Marching follows known regular graph arcs; Newton convergence at sampled points does not establish completeness.
5. Fit the 3D curve and both UV curves together, or retain a correspondence-preserving representation with bounded evaluation error. Check both surface images against the shared edge and each other across each supported segment using enclosures, not just endpoints. A geometric fit that switches branches or crosses a trim boundary fails.

CPU ownership in the early stages is an implementation placement, not permission to weaken these outputs. Later kernels can migrate culling, regular branch discovery, fitting, predicate filters and graph construction independently under the same contract.

### Trimming, classification and topology

Build an arrangement per face in its parameter charts from existing trims and newly discovered UV arcs. Split at every supported crossing, endpoint and seam event. Compute ordered directed uses and closed regions, including holes. Classification belongs to regions after subdivision; a single arbitrary original-face sample is insufficient when an intersection splits the face.

Periodic surfaces require chart lifts or explicit period offsets. Two UV locations separated by a period can refer to the same geometric seam edge, while two close 3D points can lie on different branches. Poles need a separate chart/degenerate-boundary treatment because the inverse map and normal can be singular. The upstream USD BRep known-issues list documents concrete failures involving missing/coincident torus and sphere seams, UV classification near cone poles and edge tolerance measurement on the wrong seam side. These should become targeted independent fixtures, with no assumption that the reference kernel is an infallible oracle. [Upstream known issues](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep/blob/main/KNOWN_ISSUES.md).

For Boolean selection, each arrangement cell needs an explicit inside/outside/on-boundary/ambiguous relation to the other solid, plus orientation. Coincident faces need region overlap and a declared same/opposed-normal rule for the operation. Tangent touching must be distinguished from crossing; small normal cross products only signal ambiguity. Initial outputs should be regularized manifold solids; isolated points and dangling curves remain contacts in diagnostics unless the public modeling contract explicitly supports lower-dimensional results.

Construct shared identities from originating entities and intersection events, never by a global nearest-coordinate weld. GPU count/scan/fill stages can allocate arrays; sort and group events by stable geometric/provenance keys; then build coedge cycles and adjacency. Merge candidates require compatible branch/chart/orientation information and a geometric criterion. A non-transitive tolerance proximity relation cannot serve as identity.

Emit a candidate body into separate storage. Validate its geometric compatibility, incidence, loop closure, shell orientation and supported manifoldness before replacing the input handle. Queue exhaustion, failed classifications, unresolved contacts or memory overflow return the original body and diagnostic work items. A fallback can rerun the whole operation on the CPU; mixing partial CPU and GPU outputs needs a defined shared event/identity protocol. This transactional behavior is part of the modeling semantics, not an optional debug feature.

## Attainable stages and completion conditions

| Stage | Material output | Direct completion condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0: CPU characterization | Modeling corpus, phase profiles and saved failure inputs | Analytic and metamorphic checks explain which tasks qualify and which fail; timing scope is explicit |
| 1: batched evaluation | Owned FP64 evaluation/derivative kernels with a native patch representation | Supported curve/surface identities and analytic derivatives pass; throughput and transfer-inclusive timing show actual crossover batch size |
| 2: conservative broad phase | GPU bounds and candidate patch pairs, CPU intersection/topology authority | Every analytic intersecting pair survives culling across the stated surface/domain family; ambiguous pairs are retained |
| 3: regular SSI graph | Transverse isolated branches and coupled 3D/UV curves, CPU exceptional-case fallback | Known regular components are all recovered, correspondence error is bounded and trim/seam exclusions are explicit |
| 4: owned curved Boolean | Per-face arrangements, region selection and shared-edge output for a narrow analytic/low-degree family | Set-membership and analytic geometry agree, topology invariants hold, and failures preserve inputs |
| 5: difficult geometry | Closed tiny loops, tangencies, overlap and singular/seam/pole cases added one declared family at a time | Branch completeness and classification have independent oracles for each added family; unsupported cases remain diagnosed |
| 6: GPU topology | GPU arrangement, classification and adjacency construction under the same contract | Deterministic semantic topology and geometric results across scheduling/batching; measured end-to-end modeling benefit |

No dates or speedups are assigned before phase profiles and a prototype measurement. A useful first overnight artifact is a public CPU baseline plus this scoped architecture and reading list. A full GPU kernel includes topology and classification, not just device-resident sampling.

## Hard blockers and design questions

The largest blocker is defining an attainable completeness claim for rational surface pairs under finite precision and finite work. Arbitrarily close branches, tiny loops, bad parameterizations and coincident regions defeat an unstated global epsilon. Choose an initial family with explicit degree, positive weight, regularity and separation assumptions, and return unresolved when assumptions cannot be established. Decide later whether adaptive CPU exact/symbolic work is permitted in a GPU-led kernel or whether every supported exceptional case must execute on device.

Other required decisions are tolerance-based coincidence semantics, contact/regularized-solid output semantics, chart treatment, how curve fits retain branch identity, and how multi-body common intersections share events. A hybrid mesh intermediary helps throughput but adds a mesh-resolution completeness condition; upgrading exact mesh predicates does not remove that condition. GPU scheduling/compaction is feasible engineering, while these semantics determine whether the emitted object is a solid at all.
